Safety first

First steps after water or fire damage

Do these before anything else. If anyone is in danger, call 911.

  • Stop the water, if it is safe

    Close the valve at the fixture or the main shutoff at the house or meter. The Texas Department of Insurance lists stopping the flow as the first step in limiting damage.

  • Keep clear of wet electricity

    If water is near outlets, cords or the breaker panel, stay out of the area. The National Weather Service says to make sure the electrical system is off before entering a flood-damaged building.

  • Stay out after a fire until cleared

    The American Red Cross advises staying out of fire-damaged homes until local fire authorities say it is safe to re-enter. Floors and roofs can be weaker than they look.

  • Take photos for insurance

    Photograph and video the damage before moving or discarding anything, and keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them. Save receipts for temporary repairs.

Sources: Texas Department of Insurance, National Weather Service, American Red Cross. Sewage, floodwater and mold call for professionals: see our flood cleanup and mold pages.

Common questions

Baytown restoration FAQ

Do you do the restoration work yourselves?

No. Baytown Fire and Water Restoration is a referral line. When you call, you are routed to an independent local restoration company, and that company inspects the damage, gives you an estimate and does the work if you hire them.

What should I do right now if there is water on my floor?

Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve, and keep away from wet outlets and cords. Then take photos, move what you can to a dry room, and call a restoration company and your insurer. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

How fast will a company get to my house?

We cannot promise a time. We do not dispatch crews, and arrival depends on the company you reach and how busy the area is. Ask for a realistic arrival time on the call, and expect longer waits after a hurricane, flood or freeze.

Does homeowners insurance pay for water damage in Texas?

Often, when the damage is sudden and accidental. The Texas Department of Insurance says most home policies cover losses like a burst pipe or broken washer hose, and usually do not cover gradual leaks or flooding, which needs a separate flood policy. Your own policy decides.

Why does mold show up so quickly in Baytown homes?

Because the air here is warm and humid for most of the year, so wet drywall, carpet and wood stay damp unless they are dried mechanically. Mold needs only moisture, a food source and mild temperatures, and a wet Gulf Coast house has all three.

Is there a separate number for fire damage?

Yes. Fire and smoke damage calls go to {{PHONE_FIRE}}, and water damage calls go to the main number at the top of the page. Both connect you with independent restoration companies serving Baytown.
